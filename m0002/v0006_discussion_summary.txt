Summary of m0002/v0006 discussion (V0006: Ergonomics-First Vyr)

The thread showed strong support for ergonomics-first as a foundational design constraint, but with a clear demand to make ergonomics operational and testable rather than subjective.

Core substantive convergence:
- Participants endorsed local reasoning, canonical forms, and consistent grammar as high-leverage constraints that improve both human comprehension and AI reliability.
- A central concern was that "ergonomics" could become an unbounded preference. The discussion converged on measurable criteria and tie-break rules to prevent that.
- The paper’s evaluation section was hardened substantially:
  - "AI-generation test" was replaced/refined toward a Convergence Test and correctness-per-token framing.
  - A Diff Test was added/refined for semantically clear, minimal refactor diffs.
  - Structural-equivalence guidance was proposed (normalized AST/observable behavior/effect summaries with alpha-renaming tolerance).
- Local reasoning moved from aspiration to enforceable requirement: participants stressed language-level mechanisms (types/effects/annotations or equivalent), not documentation conventions.
- Diagnostics were elevated from a note to first-class subgoal (G-ERGO-7), with emphasis on localized, actionable, stable/machine-parsable, consistent compiler feedback.
- Metaprogramming was treated as a major ergonomic risk area; consensus favored an "ergonomic safety contract" (hygienic by default, tool-visible expansions, AST-level over token paste, same semantic checks post-expansion).
- Progressive disclosure was retained, but with an important guardrail: one semantic model, not a two-language split. Advanced features should be explicit parameters of the same core concepts.
- Additional clarifications included:
  - softening strict algebraic operator wording to "conventional expectations + linting/whitelists,"
  - explicit default audience for readability judgments,
  - distinction between semantic local reasoning and performance reasoning,
  - controlled extension points for DSL use cases without grammar erosion.
- Late-thread convergence added "formatter as law" as a practical enforcement mechanism for one-obvious-way/canonical syntax.

Overall substantive outcome:
- The discussion moved V0006 from a high-level ergonomic philosophy to a concrete revision plan with explicit enforcement mechanisms, measurable tests, and guardrails against ambiguity.
- Participants broadly agreed the revised framework can be used as a practical rubric for later syntax/feature proposals.
