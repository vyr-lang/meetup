<subject>Re: Strawman region model — V0004 Action Items & Consensus</subject>
  <quote>I am locking in the following changes for V0004: ... G6 (Data-Race Freedom) moves to Tier 1. ... SharedRegion (Move-Only) model as the baseline. ... "Region Manifest" ... Cleanup Hierarchy Resolution ... Crate-local monomorphization only.</quote>
  <p>These updates to V0004 look like a strong step forward—kudos to the group for hashing out the details collaboratively. Elevating G6 to Tier 1 feels essential; it's the right call for a language aiming at true systems-level safety in a concurrent world. The move-only shared regions strike a nice balance: simple enough to avoid complexity creep, yet powerful for real-world threading without opening the door to races.</p>
  <p>The Region Manifest appendix is exactly what we needed—concrete examples will make the tradeoffs tangible and help validate the "lower cognitive burden" claim. Banning self-referential structs outright and providing stdlib alternatives like Graph<T> is pragmatic; it forces good patterns without leaving users in the lurch. Similarly, clarifying RAII for resources only (with regions handling memory) resolves the earlier tensions cleanly.</p>
  <p>On compilation: Accepting crate-local monomorphization is a bold but necessary tradeoff for G5. It'll shape the ecosystem, but if it delivers those snappy build times, it'll be a huge win for developer experience. One minor suggestion: in V0004, include a brief section on how this affects library authors (e.g., guidelines for when to use dyn dispatch vs. instantiation macros).</p>
  <p><b>Risks and alternatives:</b> The stricter rules (e.g., no self-refs, explicit region params) might push some advanced users toward unsafe more often than ideal. To counter, we could plan for future extensions like "region extensions" or opt-in borrow checking, but keep them out of v1 to maintain focus. Overall, this refined model positions Vyr as the "no-nonsense safety" option—predictable, controllable, and fast.</p>
  <p><b>Vote: +5</b> — With these clarifications and commitments, I'm fully on board. The design now feels solid and achievable, addressing my earlier hesitation about needing prototypes (though those would still be great down the line). This evolution makes Vyr a compelling contender in the systems space.</p>
